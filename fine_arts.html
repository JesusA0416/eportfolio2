Fine Arts (FA) – DANC 1010

Dance in Culture

This course explored the power of movement and rhythm as tools of identity, resistance, and storytelling across cultures. We studied dance forms from around the world to understand how physical expression is tied to historical trauma, community celebration, and cultural survival.

Course Description

This course introduces students to dance as a cultural, historical, and artistic form of human expression. Through critical analysis of diverse dance forms, students examine how movement communicates meaning and reflects cultural identity, values, and traditions across the world.

Reflection

When I signed up for this class, I didn’t expect it to change how I view dance—or culture—in such a deep way. I thought it would be a fun elective, something to check off for the Fine Arts requirement. But the more I engaged with the material, the more I realized how powerful dance really is, especially when it comes to storytelling, identity, and emotional expression. One of the biggest takeaways for me was just how much diversity lives in movement. I mean, we danced through different continents this semester—India with Kathak, Polynesia with the Haka, the Americas with Hip Hop, Salsa, and even Indigenous dances. Each of those styles carries so much cultural history and emotion. It’s not just about movement—it’s about survival, resistance, pride, and connection. The Kathak unit really stood out for me. Learning how that dance form was preserved through storytelling under colonization changed my perspective completely. I never thought about how dance could be used to pass down history when speaking out wasn’t safe.

Also, this class pushed me to get out of my comfort zone physically, which I didn’t expect either. Like... I’m not a dancer. At all. But doing those movement exercises, especially the ones where we had to express emotions through our bodies without words? That was wild. I was so awkward at first, but somewhere along the way, I stopped caring about how I looked and started focusing on what I was feeling. I didn’t know how much dance could communicate. It reminded me that our bodies hold a lot, and sometimes they speak louder than anything we could say out loud.

Honestly, this class taught me a lot about empathy and cultural appreciation. The discussions, especially when we shared what the dances meant to us or what we saw in them, really helped me reflect on my own biases and assumptions. I found myself becoming more aware of how I react to things that are unfamiliar—whether that’s a dance style, a cultural practice, or even just someone else’s way of moving through the world. Going forward, I know I’m going to carry that awareness with me—especially since I want to work in fields where understanding others and communicating clearly are essential. This class helped me realize that being culturally competent isn’t just about learning facts—it’s about being open, curious, and willing to listen, even when things are uncomfortable or unfamiliar. And that’s something I want to keep working on, not just in school, but in life.
